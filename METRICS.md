# Metrics

What to expect from a running deployment, week by week.

## Week 1

You have completed setup. You have one or two workstations and a root CLAUDE.md with your voice rules and routing map.

What this feels like: Claude opens your folder and knows your name, your role, and your communication preferences. You no longer re-explain yourself. You run the audit for the first time and it comes back clean.

| Metric | Typical value |
|---|---|
| Workstations | 1 to 3 |
| Total Markdown files | 5 to 10 |
| Skills registered | 1 to 2 |
| Time per session saved | 3 to 5 minutes of re-orientation eliminated |

The week-1 goal is not productivity. It is habituation. Getting comfortable opening the OS folder instead of a blank chat.

## Week 4

You have added workstations as you hit domains where Claude kept losing context. You have run the session-close skill a few times. MEMORY.md has grown organically.

What this feels like: Claude references a decision you made two weeks ago. It proposes a memory update before you ask. The routing map routes correctly on the first attempt.

| Metric | Typical value |
|---|---|
| Workstations | 3 to 5 |
| Total Markdown files | 15 to 25 |
| Skills registered | 2 to 4 |
| Scheduled tasks | 1 (weekly audit) |
| Audit findings per month | 2 to 5 WARNING, 0 to 1 CRITICAL |

The week-4 goal is stability. The system should feel like a colleague who knows your context, not a tool you have to configure.

## Month 3

The system has compounded. Skills handle recurring tasks consistently. The audit catches drift before it compounds. MEMORY.md entries are precise because you have learned what is worth capturing.

What this feels like: you stop noticing the OS because it just works. The sessions where you made good decisions are traceable. The ones where Claude gave weak output are diagnosable.

| Metric | Typical value |
|---|---|
| Workstations | 6 to 10 |
| Total Markdown files | 25 to 40 |
| Lines of governance | 1,500 to 3,500 |
| Skills registered | 4 to 8 |
| Scheduled tasks | 2 to 4 |
| Audit findings per month | 0 to 2 CRITICAL, 2 to 5 WARNING, 5 to 15 INFO |

CRITICAL findings should be rare at month 3. If they keep appearing, the most common cause is a routing map that has grown out of sync with actual workstations.

## What the audit catches

Concrete issues the weekly audit surfaces in practice:

- A workstation MEMORY.md that grew past its line limit and needs consolidation.
- A routing-map row pointing at a folder that was renamed or moved.
- A resource path in a workstation that broke when an external file moved.
- A skill referenced in `skills-index.md` but missing from disk.
- Two workstations whose Identity sections have drifted into overlap.

Each finding takes 5 to 15 minutes to fix once surfaced. None would be noticed without the audit.

## Time investment

| Activity | Time |
|---|---|
| Initial setup | 30 to 45 minutes |
| Adding a workstation | 15 to 30 minutes |
| Weekly audit review | 5 to 10 minutes |
| Session close | 3 to 5 minutes |
| Monthly memory consolidation | 20 to 40 minutes |

Ongoing maintenance settles at roughly 20 to 30 minutes per week once the system is mature.

## What this does not measure

The system does not track satisfaction or output quality. Those are yours to judge. The metrics above are about the system itself: its structure, its drift rate, its time cost. Whether it makes you more effective is a question you answer after a few months, not a few days.
