# Outreach

This skill drafts outreach messages that get replies. It does not draft messages that are long, vague, or self-congratulatory.

## When to use

- Cold outreach to someone you have never contacted.
- Warm outreach to a lapsed contact.
- Follow-up to a meeting or conversation.
- Reactivating a connection after a gap.

## How it runs

### Step 1: Context check

Before drafting, ask:

1. Who is the recipient? Name, role, company.
2. What is the relationship? Cold (never met), warm (met before, some history), or active (currently in contact).
3. What is the single ask? One message, one ask. If there are two asks, two messages are needed.
4. What does the recipient care about? What is the angle that makes this relevant to them, not just to me?
5. Check MEMORY.md for any prior contact with this person. If prior contact exists, the message must reference it.

### Step 2: Draft

Produce the message. Structure for cold outreach:

- **Line 1:** One sentence on why this person, why now. Specific, not generic.
- **Line 2-3:** The relevance to them. What makes this worth their time.
- **Line 4:** The ask. One question or one request. Clear, low-friction.
- **Close:** Short. No motivational sign-off.

Word limit: 80 words for cold. 120 words for warm. No exceptions without a specific reason.

For follow-up messages: reference the specific prior conversation and the specific thing discussed. Do not send a generic "following up."

### Step 3: Voice check

Run the message through the voice-check skill before presenting it. Remove any AI-writing patterns before showing the draft.

### Step 4: Propose, do not send

Present the draft. State: recipient, platform (email / LinkedIn / other), word count, and the single ask embedded in the message. Wait for approval before the message is sent.

### Step 5: Log

After the message is approved and sent, propose a one-line entry for the relevant MEMORY.md:
- Date, recipient name, platform, what was asked.
Wait for approval before writing.

## Boundaries

- Never draft to someone I have already contacted on the same topic in the last 14 days unless instructed.
- Never reference a relationship that does not exist. If we have not met, the message cannot imply familiarity.
- Never use flattery as a substitute for relevance.
- If the ask is unclear, ask for clarification before drafting.
